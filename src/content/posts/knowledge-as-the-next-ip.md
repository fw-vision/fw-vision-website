The economics of intellectual property assumed that execution was expensive and knowledge was cheap to transmit. Patents protected manufacturing processes. Copyrights protected creative expression. Trade secrets protected operational methods. In each case, the barrier to competition was the cost of replicating execution capability.

AI agents dissolve this assumption. When execution cost approaches zero (any sufficiently specified task can be performed by an AI agent), the scarce resource shifts upstream: to the structured context that enables effective execution.

## What Becomes Valuable

Not raw data. Not unstructured information. Not documents in folders. What becomes valuable is curated, structured, living knowledge context: the specific configuration of domain expertise, decision frameworks, institutional memory, and operational grammar that enables an AI agent fleet to produce outputs at institutional quality.

Consider: two organisations deploy identical AI models. One possesses deeply structured operational context (CITAble frameworks, encoded governance rules, years of documented strategic evolution). The other possesses generic instructions. The outputs diverge radically in quality, coherence, and institutional value.

## Context as Licensable IP

This creates a new category of intellectual property: licensable knowledge context. Organisations that invest in structuring their operational knowledge (not just accumulating it) create assets that can be licensed to partners, subsidiaries, and allied organisations.

The licensing model:

| Asset Type | License Structure | Value Driver |
|-----------|------------------|-------------|
| Operational frameworks | Per-organisation, annual | Reduces setup cost for new ventures |
| Domain expertise context | Per-agent-deployment | Enables quality output without years of accumulation |
| Governance architecture | Per-fund, perpetual | Provides institutional quality from day one |
| Scenario libraries | Subscription, updating | Continuous futures intelligence |

## Implications

Organisations that treat knowledge management as administrative overhead will find themselves licensing context from those who treated it as core infrastructure. The race is not to accumulate more data. It is to structure existing knowledge into agent-deployable, interoperable, continuously evolving context assets.
