## Definition

**Horizons of Concern** are the time bands FW.VISION uses to place futures work in time. They give every scenario and trajectory a temporal address, so a storyline can be read and searched by when its futures land.

## The Four Horizons

| Horizon | Span | Character |
|---------|------|-----------|
| **H1** | 3 to 10 years | Near term; building now, the provable steps |
| **H2** | 10 to 30 years | Medium term; scaling, coalitions reach density |
| **H3** | 30 to 50 years | Long term; structural, sovereignty-level outcomes |
| **H4** | 100 years and beyond | Civilisational; species-scale ambitions |

## Intermediate Horizons

Futures rarely fall on clean band edges. Intermediate points are expressed with a decimal: **H1.5**, **H2.5**, **H3.5**. A scenario that sits between the medium and long term (roughly 20 to 35 years out) is H2.5. This keeps the language precise without multiplying bands.

## Why Horizons Matter

- **Searchability.** Trajectory storylines are searchable by horizon of concern: a reader can ask what FW.VISION studies at H4 and find the civilisational trajectories (First Light, Second Light) distinct from the near-term work at H1.
- **Trajectory shape.** A preferred trajectory threads through horizons: present, then H1 proof, then H2 scaling, out to an H3 or H4 ambition.
- **Rigour.** Placing a claim in a horizon forces honesty about when it could be true, separating a 5-year plan from a 100-year aspiration.

## Relationship to the Foresight Scope

The Foresight Scope classifies scenarios by likelihood (the four P's); Horizons of Concern classify them by time. Together they locate any scenario on two axes: how probable, and how far away. The ForesightScope tool renders horizons as nested cross-sections along the futures cone.
