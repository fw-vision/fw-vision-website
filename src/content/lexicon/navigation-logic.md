## Definition

**Navigation Logic** is the roadmap that specifies what must change to keep a preferred trajectory on course despite severe constraints. Where a [[Scenario Off-Ramp]] names where a trajectory fails, navigation logic names what to do about it: the specific innovations, partnerships, and policy shifts that steer around the off-ramp.

## Mechanism

1. **Trajectory definition.** State the ambition clearly, within its real constraints.
2. **Off-ramp identification.** Mark the junctions where the trajectory fails.
3. **Required innovations.** Specify what must change (technical, organisational, political) to avoid each off-ramp.
4. **Implementation sequence.** Order the interventions for maximum effect.

## From Question to Priority

Navigation logic converts the compelling questions that surface off-ramps into an actionable set of priorities. A "Can if" framing is the bridge: a constraint that looks fatal becomes "we can reach this future if the following changes occur." Those "if" conditions are the navigation logic.

## Why It Matters

Foresight without navigation logic is vision without a route. Navigation logic is what makes a preferred trajectory a plan rather than a hope, and it is the layer FW.VISION hands to the operational partners (SyncID Labs and the ventures it hosts) who actualize the futures. It is Active Futuring made concrete: the specific present actions that move toward the chosen future rather than waiting for it.
