## Definition

A **Scenario Off-Ramp** is a precise junction where a preferred trajectory goes off the rails. It is a constraint that, left unaddressed, diverts development away from the desired future and into an alternative, unwelcome scenario.

Off-ramps are what make a trajectory storyline honest. A preferred path that names no off-ramps is a wish. A path that maps its off-ramps is an argument that can be pressure-tested.

## Mechanism

1. **Constraint identification.** Find the severe limitations that define the playing field.
2. **Off-ramp mapping.** Locate exactly where the trajectory fails if a constraint is not addressed.
3. **Failure scenarios.** Describe the alternative futures that emerge from taking the off-ramp.
4. **Navigation planning.** Specify the interventions that keep the trajectory on course (see [[Navigation Logic]]).

## How Off-Ramps Are Found

Off-ramps are surfaced by **compelling questions**: the questions that, if answered wrongly or ignored, break the trajectory. Pre-mortem analysis (imagining the failure and working backward) and red-teaming (attacking the strategy to find where it snaps) are the practical techniques.

## Why They Belong in Every Storyline

The contrast between a preferred trajectory and its off-ramp failure cases is the analytical payload of a storyline. Showing how a single unaddressed constraint divides the future's development, one path to the ambition, another to collapse, is more instructive than any single optimistic scenario. This is why a storyline may hold up to three Foresight Scopes: to render that contrast explicitly.
