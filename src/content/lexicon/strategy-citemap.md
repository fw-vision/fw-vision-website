## Definition

The Strategy CITEMap is the visual instrument panel for a CITAble organisational structure. Where CITAble defines what information exists and where it lives, the CITEMap provides a banded quadrant view that renders relationships, progress, temporal evolution, and scenario contingencies in a single navigable interface.

## What the CITEMap Renders

| Dimension | Visualisation |
|-----------|--------------|
| Band relationships | How items in Context, Indicators, Tactics, and Efforts relate to each other |
| Progress states | Draft, in-progress, review, published across all efforts |
| Temporal evolution | How strategy shifted over time; decision archaeology |
| Scenario simulation | What happens if an indicator changes; cascade visibility |
| Dependency chains | Which tactics depend on which strategic indicators |

## Relationship to CITAble

CITAble is the framework. Strategy CITEMap is the instrument panel. The distinction matters: the framework defines structural grammar (where information lives, how it relates, what cadence it follows). The CITEMap renders that structure visually for human navigation and strategic decision-making.

## Design Properties

The CITEMap treats time as a first-class citizen. Traditional strategy tools present a point-in-time snapshot. The CITEMap preserves the full arc: what the context looked like when a strategy was formulated, what signals prompted revision, which tactics succeeded or failed, and what efforts accumulated.

This temporal depth serves three functions:

1. **Honest retrospection.** Organisations can trace why decisions were made with the information available at the time.
2. **Knowledge succession.** New leaders or advisors can orient rapidly by reading the strategic evolution.
3. **Pattern recognition.** Recurring dynamics (strategies that always stall at the same point, indicators that precede tactical failure) become visible across time.

## Application

Strategic fund managers use the CITEMap to evaluate governance quality of portfolio organisations. Policy architects use it to trace how institutional strategy responds to environmental shifts. Founder-led organisations use it as their primary strategic navigation interface, replacing static slide decks and quarterly reports with a living, interactive system.
