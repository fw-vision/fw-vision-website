## Definition

The **Cognitive Vitality Index (CVI)** measures the cognition of an organisation: its capacity to sense its environment, reason about it, decide well, and learn from outcomes. It treats a business as a thinking system and asks how alive that thinking is.

CVI is one of the two axes of Hybrid Intelligence. It describes the human-centric side of the equation: the quality of judgment, attention, and sense-making that governs the enterprise.

## Input, Output, Ratio

CVI follows a structure familiar from innovation indices: it separates the inputs an organisation invests in cognition from the outputs it produces, and reads the ratio between them.

- **Inputs**: attention, deliberate reflection, decision hygiene, sensing infrastructure.
- **Outputs**: quality decisions, learning velocity, adaptive moves.
- **Ratio**: cognitive efficiency, how much thinking capacity converts into good judgment.

A high-input, low-output organisation is busy but not vital. CVI surfaces that distinction.

## Why It Pairs with the Agentic Gradient

Cognition alone does not scale. The Agentic Gradient measures how much of the organisation's volume is carried by AI fleets. CVI x Agentic Gradient is the precise definition of Hybrid Intelligence: high cognitive vitality (humans at the centre, governing and judging) meeting strong agentic integration (AI carrying the scale). The ideal is not maximal automation; it is human-centric, agentic scale.

## Role in the CITAble Business Index

CVI feeds the CITAble Business Index as the cognition reading of the four-quadrant alignment. A future-proof venture must think well, not just automate heavily. CVI is how that thinking is measured and made citable.
